01 // Problem Context

The Cold Chain Spoilage Crisis

Why traditional post-harvest supply chain tracking fails fresh produce

1/3 of Food Wasted

Over 30% to 40% of agricultural fresh produce is wasted globally before reaching consumers, driven primarily by unseen temperature spikes and respiratory breakdown during transit.

  • $1 Trillion annual economic losses
  • Unnecessary carbon footprint
Static Expiration Dates

Traditional 'Best Before' dates are static and arbitrary. They ignore actual logistics delays, container ventilation failures, and temperature swings encountered in transit.

  • No adaptation to heat spikes
  • Causes premature discard of fresh food
The IoT-AI Opportunity

Combining ambient wireless sensors (Temp, Humidity, Light, CO2) with machine learning allows operators to predict quality degradation hours before physical rot occurs.

  • Proactive atmosphere adjustment
  • Automated cold chain routing

03 // Biophysical Sensors

Environmental Sensors & Features

10,996 curated observations capturing key produce degradation biomarkers

Storage Temperature Temp (°C)

The primary catalyst for microbial multiplication and enzymatic breakdown. Every 2–3°C rise above optimal limits doubles produce respiration speed.

Relative Humidity Humid (%)

Controls cellular transpiration. Low humidity causes moisture loss and wilting; saturated conditions (>95%) facilitate fungal spore germination.

Ambient Light Exposure Light (Lux)

Triggers photo-oxidation, chlorophyll breakdown, and surface discolouration while generating localized packaging micro-climates.

Carbon Dioxide Output CO2 (ppm)

Direct biomarker of cellular respiration. Rising CO2 concentrations indicate rapid tissue metabolism, fermentative breakdown, and imminent spoilage.

04 // Data Sanitation

Cleaning & Outlier Management

Transforming messy sensor telemetry into high-fidelity training vectors

Casing Harmonization

Raw datasets contained inconsistencies such as BAD, Bad, and trailing whitespace. The data loader automatically sanitizes all labels to title-case binary targets (Good vs. Bad).

Sensor Outlier Clipping

Mitigates anomalous sensor glitches by applying Interquartile Range (IQR) clipping on CO2 readings: [Q1 - 1.5*IQR, Q3 + 1.5*IQR], preserving genuine data distributions.

Stratified Splitting

Uses stratified 80/20 train-test partitioning to guarantee identical class distribution across training (8,796 rows) and test (2,200 rows) partitions, preventing class imbalance skew.

05 // Exploratory Data Analysis

Statistical & Biophysical Insights

Uncovering core correlations driving produce degradation

Thermal-Respiration Coupling

Strong positive correlation between Temperature and CO2 emissions. Elevated temperatures exponentially accelerate respiration, identifying this pairing as the primary spoil predictor.

Key Threshold: Temperature > 24°C and CO2 > 370 ppm indicates an 88% probability of produce quality failure.
Multi-Variety Behavioral Parity

The dataset provides balanced observations across Orange, Banana, Pineapple, and Tomato. Each variety exhibits varying sensitivity to ambient humidity and packaging light exposure.

Visual Engine: ml/eda.py renders automated dark-themed PNG charts into static/charts/ for immediate operator visualization.
06 // Feature Transformation

Modular ColumnTransformer

Guaranteed zero training-serving skew with encapsulated pipelines

StandardScaler (Sensors)

Continuous sensors (Temp, Humidity, Light, CO2) are standardized to zero mean and unit variance (μ=0, σ=1). Prevents high-magnitude CO2 values (>400) from overpowering lower-magnitude temperature readings (20–26°C).

StandardScaler().fit_transform(numeric_cols)
OneHotEncoder (Produce)

Produce varieties are mapped to binary dummy variables. Fitted with handle_unknown='ignore' to ensure live prediction inference never faults on unseen inputs.

OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown='ignore')

Actionable Decision Guidance

Rather than delivering a raw binary class, FreshChain generates human-readable diagnostics and operational remediation advice:

  • Good: "Optimal conditions maintained. Produce is fresh and fit for transit."
  • Bad: "Suboptimal environment! Immediate atmospheric ventilation or refrigeration adjustment recommended."
